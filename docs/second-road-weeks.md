# Second Road — four week-summaries (s0, the go/no-go)

Written 2026-08-07. These are the artifact the whole project turns on: if a week
at macro scale reads as well as a good night at the Stinky, Second Road is worth
building. If it reads like a report, it isn't.

Each has to do **two** jobs — tell you what happened, and tempt you into a night.
The drop-in hook is the last line of each, and it should feel like an invitation
rather than a menu item.

Voice is LBB's: second person, grounded, specific, unsentimental. No numbers the
prose doesn't need; the books are a separate screen.

---

## Week 41 · High season · The good one

Somebody put it about that the Stinky does a proper Sunday roast now, which is
not true and has never been true, and by Wednesday you had served forty of them.
Bert sourced the beef himself from a man in Bang Lamung and has said the word
"gravy" more times this week than in the preceding eleven years.

The rail filled every night before nine. Not the sprawling, spending, two-week
kind of full — the other kind, where the same fourteen people arrive at
roughly the same time out of habit and nobody has to be sold anything. Nong
worked five nights and was the reason for at least three of them. She knows it.
She has not yet worked out how to ask you for anything on the strength of it,
and when she does you will say yes.

The month's payment went to Ohio on Tuesday. It went from the till, which it has
not always done.

One flat note: the Shamrock's roof is still open to the sky at the back and the
rains are six weeks out, and the man Tan recommended has now not come twice.

## Week 44 · High season · The bad one

The licence renewal came back requiring a document nobody has ever produced,
from an office that keeps its own hours. You spent Monday and most of Wednesday
on it. The bar ran fine without you both nights, which was reassuring for about
an hour and then wasn't.

Manow gave notice on Thursday. Not for money — she was very clear about that,
and slightly offended you suggested it — but because her sister has a shop in
Ubon and needs somebody who can count. She'll work out the month. She has been
here since before you owned the place and she trained the two girls you'd have
promoted instead of her.

Then Saturday: a table of six ran a tab to four thousand baht and simply
weren't there at one. Bert saw it coming twenty minutes out and couldn't stop it
without making a scene in front of a full room, which is exactly what they were
counting on. He has taken it personally in the way he takes everything
personally, quietly, and will not mention it again.

Takings held. That is the strange part. The week was a disaster and the numbers
are fine, and you have not decided yet whether that is a comfort.

> **Manow's last Friday is this week.** The girls are planning something and
> nobody will tell you what. *(Drop in →)*

## Week 19 · Low season · The long one

Rain from Tuesday. Not the dramatic kind — the other kind, the grey persistent
kind that doesn't stop the day so much as make it pointless, and that empties a
soi more completely than any storm.

Three good nights, four where you'd have done better closing. Ao spent Thursday
folding the same stack of towels twice because there was nothing else and she
wanted to look busy, and you didn't tell her to stop, because she wanted to look
busy. You put the football on for two Norwegians and one of them stayed until
close and talked about his divorce, and he tipped like a man who'd been listened
to, which he had.

You paid the old man from the till and the till didn't quite cover it, so the
rest came from your pocket. Nobody saw you do it. That's most of the job.

The Shamrock roof is done. Four months early, which in this town is a form of
apology.

Four months of this. You knew that when you bought it — everybody tells you, and
you nod, and then you find out what four months means.

> **Wednesday is Wan Phra.** No alcohol, doors shut, and the girls have asked
> if they can use the place for a birthday anyway. *(Drop in →)*

## Week 63 · Shoulder season · The one about trust

You have known for three weeks. You would like the record to show that you knew
for three weeks and did nothing, because that is the part worth being honest
about.

Ploy has been running the Shamrock since March and running it well — better
than you did, which you have said out loud to people and meant. The takings are
up, the girls stay, the Thursday crowd is hers and not yours. And the float has
been light on Sundays since April, by two to three thousand, in a pattern too
regular to be error and too small to be greed.

The thing nobody tells you is that there are two kinds, and they don't look
different from outside. There's the kind where a manager takes a little because
that is understood to be part of the wage, everywhere, always, and the bar runs
better for her feeling that it is partly hers. And there's the kind that starts
at two thousand.

Bert thinks you should say nothing. Candy thinks you should say something now,
lightly, once, and never again — and that if you cannot do it lightly you should
not do it at all. Tan, asked directly, said: *"She is very good. Good is
expensive, my friend. You are paying for it one way. Do you want to pay a
different way?"* — and then changed the subject, entirely warmly, and you have
been chewing on it since.

She has done nothing to the bar. The bar is the best it has been.

> **You're expected at the Shamrock on Saturday.** She'll have the float ready
> and the books open, the way she always does. *(Drop in →)*

## Assessment

Reading these back, the honest verdict:

**They work, and I can say why.** A week has the same shape as a good LBB night —
a specific concrete detail (gravy, folded towels twice, the float light by two
thousand on Sundays), a person doing something you can picture, and a closing
line that turns rather than concludes. The macro scale doesn't flatten the voice
because the *unit* changed while the register didn't: a night reports moments, a
week reports decisions and their weather.

**What the scale buys that a night can't:** week 63 is not writable in LBB. It
needs three weeks of knowing, a pattern in a float, and a manager who has become
better at your job than you were. That's the argument for the whole project.

**The one that worries me** is week 41, the good one. Good weeks are the hardest
to make interesting, and it leans on the Shamrock's unfinished roof for tension —
i.e. it borrows a bad thing to be readable. Worth watching: if every good week
needs a flat note to work, the game will feel relentlessly grim, and the fix is
that a good week has to be allowed to just be good sometimes.

**The drop-in hooks pull.** All four made me want to play the night, and week 44's
(the girls planning something for Manow's last Friday, and nobody will say what)
is the strongest — because it's the one where I have no idea what would happen
and the macro layer genuinely cannot tell me.

**Verdict: GO**, with the caveat above about good weeks.
